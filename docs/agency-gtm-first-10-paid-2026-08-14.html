1The short answer

We don't have a "nobody's looking at us" problem. We have two smaller problems that happen to multiply together.

Problem one: agencies open our emails and then do nothing. 43 agencies were emailed. 17 opened them — that's a genuinely good open rate. Not one replied.

Problem two: the few agencies who do sign up use the product once and never return. Six signed up. Five ran a single search and disappeared. Nobody has paid us anything.

And underneath both: there are only about 1,200 agencies of the type we're targeting. Even if everything worked perfectly, that's not enough people to get to 10 customers. We have to widen who we email.

The important detail. Every one of those searches finished successfully. Nothing crashed. The product worked and they still left. That means they either didn't like what they got back, or nothing ever reminded them to come back.

Three things are missing at exactly the moments that matter:

1

2

Two agencies used every last credit — which means they were genuinely interested — and at that exact moment the product showed them no way to buy more. They just left.

3
Nobody ever emails them again.

Someone signs up, tries it once, gets busy, forgets. No lifecycle email exists to bring them back. Five people needed one nudge and got none.

4The counting problem

This one has nothing to do with copywriting, and it's the reason the current plan can't work.

We chose to target US dental and med-spa marketing specialists. There are roughly 1,200 of them in the world. That's the pool.

So: emailing this group cannot get us to 10 customers. Not with better writing, not with more follow-ups. There aren't enough of them. We run out of people before we run out of luck.
The fix is one decision. Stop targeting only dental and med-spa specialists. Target every US marketing agency that serves local businesses — roughly 12,000 instead of 1,200. Ten times the pool, same product, same emails.

When we tested this, it was the difference between reaching 10 customers in almost none of our simulations and reaching it in 37% of them. It's the single highest-value decision in this document.
One more mismatch worth knowing. We email American agencies, but our best data is Canadian — we have 2,963 Canadian dental businesses and only 158 American ones. So when an American agency signs up and searches their own city, they often get thin results. That is very likely why five of six never came back.

5Something breaking right now

We have 2,144 pages on the site, one per business, and Google has them all indexed. In late July they started getting real impressions — up to 271 a day. Then on 4 August they fell off a cliff, down to under 20 a day, and they've stayed there. Nothing alerted us. We found it today.

But here's the harder truth. Even at full strength, this traffic was the wrong people. Those pages come up when someone searches "iboma center reviews" — that's a customer looking up a med spa, not an agency looking for leads. Customers of med spas will never buy our tool.

The pages aren't useless — the data behind them is excellent. They're just answering the wrong question. Point that same data at what an agency would type and it becomes valuable.

8The recommended plan

Seven small things, all running by themselves. Reaches 10 paying agencies in 83% of simulations, usually around month 5. Costs you about 2 hours a week and $462 for the year. No advertising spend, no daily manual work.

First 3 weeks — stop the leaks

Do these before anything else. Every visitor the later steps bring in is wasted until these are fixed.

1
Build a pricing page

It's currently an error page. Nothing else works until someone can find out what we charge.

2
Show a "buy more" screen when free credits run out

The moment someone is most interested is the moment we currently show them nothing.

3
Send a few emails after someone signs up

Three over ten days. Five agencies needed exactly this and got nothing.

4
Widen who we email

All US agencies serving local businesses, not just dental and med-spa. 10× the pool.

5
Add an alert when Google traffic drops

So the next 4 August doesn't go unnoticed for ten days.

Weeks 2–8 — make people stick

6
Send the report instead of asking to send it

Delete the question that got 0 replies from 43 people. Lead with the link.

7
Make the first market report genuinely free

Charge when they export the leads, not when they look. On its own this plan reached the goal 71% of the time.

8
Steer the first search to a city we know well

So nobody's first impression is an empty screen.

9
Turn shared reports into a referral loop

16 reports have been shared already. None of them invite anyone or track where they went.

Weeks 5–16 — build the engine that compounds

10
Auto-build pages for what agencies search

We have data for thousands. Right now not one page targets an agency's question.

11
More "Mapsly vs …" comparison pages

Three exist. They're the pages closest to a purchase decision.

12
Get quoted by ChatGPT and Perplexity

A lot of research starts there in 2026 and almost nobody is competing for it yet.

13
Put Mapsly in GoHighLevel's app store

Slowest to build, best fit on the list. Tens of thousands of agencies already work in there daily.

Where the numbers come from. Everything about our own performance was read directly from the live database on 14 August 2026 — users, agencies, credits, email campaigns, opens, clicks, searches run. Google figures come from Search Console (1 June – 13 August) and Google Analytics. The simulations ran 20,000 times per plan over 52 weeks, varying every uncertain rate on each run.

How much to trust it. The direction is solid; the exact percentages are not. Everything after "someone signs up" is based on six agencies and zero paying customers, so those numbers carry wide margins.
