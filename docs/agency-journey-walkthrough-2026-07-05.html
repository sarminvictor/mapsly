The short version

The path works, the data is real and honestly priced — one core signal is the missing piece.

A brand-new agency can go from landing page to a signal-grounded, compliance-safe outreach draft in one sitting, spending about a dollar. Discovery is free and transparent, billing only charges for what's delivered (and refunds what isn't), and the enriched data — contacts, reviews, tech, ads, rank, an AI brief — is genuinely usable. The gap is that the one signal the chosen goal is built on doesn't resolve, so the "qualify them by the exact problem you fix" promise lands only half-way for this hunt.

Would a real agency owner finish this hunt and feel it was worth it? Yes — but they'd ask why every lead scores 100%.

They'd get 44 mapped med spas for free, 3 fully researched for ~$1, a shortlist with real reasons (0% reply rate, 5 active Google ads, no online booking, a 35/100 site), and a phone script that opens on a verifiable pain. What they couldn't do is rank the market by "who's overdue for a redesign" — the very filter they picked — because that verdict never computes.

The scenario that had to succeed

"From free leads to a quality touch, nothing failing, data ready to use" — did it hold?

This was the bar: a new agency goes landing → register → map a small market → research → compare → understand the strategy → build a quality touch, with data that's transparent, trustworthy, and ready. Here's the honest scorecard on that exact promise.

✓ What held up

The full path completed in one sitting for ~$2.55. Discovery was free and honest; billing charged only for delivered data and auto-refunded 16 credits for sites it couldn't reach and a Meta scan that came back empty. The researched fields — contacts, socials, CMS, reviews with reply rate, Google-ads count, organic rank, an AI positioning brief — are real and specific. The two leads were clearly differentiated. The touch was signal-grounded and compliance-safe by construction, and slotted into a real pipeline. A first-timer ends the session with a usable shortlist and a draft they'd send.

✕ Where "ready to use" falls short

One thing failed the "nothing should fail" bar, and it's the load-bearing one: the signal the whole hunt was chosen for — "Overdue for a redesign" — never resolves. Site speed is enriched and clearly differs (35/100 CWV-failing vs 65/100 CWV-ok), yet both leads show that verdict as "Enrich to unlock," so every lead reads "Match 100%" and the workbench can't rank or filter by the exact problem the agency sells against. Combined with a couple of trust wobbles — a two-tier credit model that isn't explained, "spend to date: 0" while the wallet visibly drops, and an email touch that no-ops silently — the run is ~80% of the promise: the evidence is there and honest, but the product doesn't yet turn it into the ranked, qualified shortlist the pitch promises.

The question this run answers

Are we sure every research actually fires and returns quality data?

Run 1 proved the path. It didn't prove that all seven data groups produce real data — Kelowna's med spas happened to exercise most, but not on demand and not head-to-head. So I ran a second, deliberately different hunt: a fresh US market (Boise, Idaho), a small niche (acupuncture clinics), the Website-redesign goal, and then — the point of the exercise — every one of the seven data groups turned on at once on three leads. One lead, Meridian Family Acupuncture, is dissected field-by-field below.

Why a second market at all

To separate "this research works" from "this market happened to have data." Boise acupuncture clinics are small and mostly ad-free — the honest failure modes ("ran, found nothing") show up cleanly, and any research that can't deliver has nowhere to hide.

How every research got exercised

In the workbench, selecting leads opens an Enrich-more popup with all seven groups and a Select all. Turning them all on and running is the only way to fire Contacts, Reviews, Site-speed, AI brief + Services, Meta ads, Google ads and Search rank together. Total for 3 leads: ~$1.10.

◆ The one research that doesn't deliver — Meta ads

Across two independent markets (Kelowna med spas, Boise acupuncture) the Meta Ad-Library scan returned no data. It fails honestly — the field reads "NOT ENRICHED", the credits refund automatically, and the un-merge means it never masquerades as Google ads' "none found" — but for an agency selling paid-social audits, this signal is currently unusable. The actor is the blocker, not the app. This is the single highest-value fix left in the enrichment stack.

◆ Reproduced on fresh data — the goal signal still won't resolve

Meridian has its Site-speed data (39/100, CWV failing) yet the goal's own signal, "Overdue for a redesign," shows "Enrich to unlock" in Why this lead qualifies while "Operating business" and "Has a website" read Fired. The signal isn't wired to compute from the site-speed data the user already paid for. This is the same P0 as Run 1 — now reproduced on a different market, so it's a logic gap, not a Kelowna fluke. It's why every lead still scores 100% on match.

New findings the second path surfaced

Three things the fresh setup exposed that Run 1 didn't

Starting cold — a new city, a Custom goal, the cap slider — walked through corners the Kelowna path skipped. Two are real defects; one is a friction.

Sev Finding Fix
P1 A goal with no research-backed signal can't be enriched — and leaks a raw error. A Custom goal of only discovery-data signals (operating business + has a website) previews at "~0 credits · no enrichment research," and clicking Enrich returns the raw Zod string "Array must contain at least 1 element(s)". The request ships an empty research array; validation rejects it and the internal message reaches the user. P2 The sticky "Enrich →" doubles as a scroll control on the preview. On a long preview it took several clicks — each scrolling to the next review section (sample → what-you-picked → what-it-costs) — before it actually fired, reading as a stuck button. Keep the review-before-spend nudge, but make the first click either act or visibly say "review the cost below, then confirm." preview · sticky bar
