The one-paragraph verdict. The billing machine is excellent — hold/settle/refund, anti-tamper re-quotes, idempotency everywhere (9/10 infrastructure). The billing economics are broken: after WP10-7 "honest cost re-derivation," the price list now charges users our raw vendor cost, rounded up to $0.05 granularity. Four enrichment SKUs have literally 0% margin, two go negative on their tails, and at plan-effective credit rates the Scale tier sells data at ~25% of vendor cost. Meanwhile the market (Apollo, Origami, Clay) charges 6–30× more per enriched lead than we do. One P0 Stripe bug on top: a $299 Scale buyer would be granted 900 Starter credits. The fix is not a rewrite — it's one decision: separate what we track (COGS) from what we charge (credits) — and the marketing cards already advertise the correct 3-credits-per-lead pricing that settle just fails to charge.

1 · Your question: is a full enrichment 1 credit?

The code has three different answers, and that's the root of the confusion.

Definition Where What it says a lead costs
Marketing definition (CREDIT_MEANING) pricing.ts:141-148 · PLAN_CARDS 1 credit = lead with contacts · 3 credits = fully-enriched lead · 10 credits per 100 first-touch messages. The plan cards' "$0.05 / enriched lead" math is derived from this.
Settle definition (what's actually charged) estimate.ts:90-93 · dispatch.ts:1097 ceil(actual vendor USD ÷ $0.05) per run. A fully-enriched open-site lead ≈ $0.0403 → ~1 credit. Your Booking-goal example: 84 leads × (contacts $0.008 + tech $0.001) = $0.756 → 16 credits ≈ 0.19 credits/lead.
Slider definition (the UI's per-lead rate) flow-types.ts:201-208 ceil($0.009 ÷ $0.05) = 1 credit/lead — every lead rounded up to a whole credit. 5.6× pessimistic vs the real charge. This is why your slider defaulted to 26 of 84 when 26 credits actually covered all 84 (true cost 16).

So for your live example (goal "Booking tool" → signals Operating business + Has a website [both free at discovery] + No online booking tool [needs the Website & tech DOM scan]):

  • The tech fingerprint only exists as a byproduct of the contacts DOM fetch (researches.ts:56-73 — the one dependency chain in the system), so contacts rides along mechanically: 14 cr contacts + 2 cr tech = 16 cr for 84 sites.
  • No, a full enrichment is not 1 credit today. Today it's ~1 credit by accident of rounding cost pass-through, while the cards advertise 3, and the slider pretends 1-per-lead. Three sources of truth, all disagreeing. Section 10 proposes making the marketing definition the real one.

The lifecycle (all of this is genuinely well built)

  1. Quote — preflight mints a single-use 15-min CostEstimate; inputs persisted server-side.
  2. Authorize — server re-derives the price from persisted inputs; >10% drift or price-list bump forces re-quote. Client numbers are never trusted. server.ts:148-229
  3. Hold — atomic conditional UPDATE (WHERE available ≥ n) closes the concurrent-oversell race. server.ts:461-485
  4. Run — actions only enqueue; the run action takes only the estimateId and reconstructs scope server-side (anti-tamper).
  5. Settle — bills actual, clamped to the hold (never charges above it), refunds the difference; zero-progress runs fully refunded; idempotent. Draw-down: plan → rollover → purchased. Rollover capped at 3× monthly grant; purchased credits never expire. server.ts:517-616

Freshness dedup is real and generous: fresh units are $0 to the user and ~$0 to us (skipped, no vendor call). Windows: contacts/services/reviews 90d · tech/lighthouse/ai/cells 30d · discovery 182d. Failed jobs bill $0 — we already have "charge only on success," which Origami and Clay both market loudly. We don't market it at all.

3 · Price list vs real vendor cost (COGS)

The crux: ENRICHMENT_PRICES.usdPerUnit is raw expected vendor cost — WP10-7 deliberately re-derived it from the real call graph (great for cost telemetry) and in doing so deleted the markup that used to exist (google_ads was 13× higher, serp 3.7× higher). Settle bills ceil(Σ plannedUsd / $0.05). There is no markup constant anywhere in the system. Whatever margin exists is an accident of the $0.05 rounding granularity plus the gap between credit face value and plan-effective rates.

The structural mistake: one table is doing two jobs. ENRICHMENT_PRICES is superb COGS telemetry (WP10-7 made it honest) — but it's also the customer price list, so honesty about cost became zero margin for the business. Every healthy competitor separates these: Clay's marketplace data carries a 50–80% margin over vendor cost; Origami takes margin on research credits while running verification at cost; ZoomInfo runs an 87% gross margin.

4 · Where we lose money — worked examples

Example 1 · Growth customer, 2,000 fully-enriched leads/mo

Settle: ceil(2,000 × $0.0403 / $0.05) = 1,610 credits — barely a quarter of their 6,000-credit grant.

Revenue consumed: 1,610 × $0.0165 = $26.57
Vendor COGS: 2,000 × $0.013 avg = $26.00 → margin ≈ 2%
With a realistic walled/deep-review tail ($0.02/lead): COGS $40 → loss −$13

Same usage after the Section-10 fix (3–4 cr/lead): 6,000–8,000 credits consumed → $99 revenue vs $26–40 COGS → 60–74% gross margin.

Example 2 · Scale customer, bulk reviews on 1,000 leads

Settle: 1,000 × $0.015 = $15 → 300 credits.

Revenue consumed: 300 × $0.0125 = $3.75
Vendor COGS: $15.00 (more if the depth ladder escalates)
→ loss −$11.25 per run, repeatable every 90 days

At Scale's $0.0125/credit, every pass-through SKU (reviews, lighthouse, serp, google_ads) sells at ~25% of what it costs us.

Why this hasn't hurt yet: dev mode, no users. Why it will: the heaviest users gravitate to exactly the bulk patterns above, and plan-effective rates reward them for it. A solo founder's budget cannot absorb negative-margin whales.

10 · Proposal: value-based credit schedule

One principle: keep ENRICHMENT_PRICES exactly as it is — as the COGS table (telemetry, upper bounds, cost audit). Add a second, tiny table — CREDIT_PRICES — in whole credits per unit, and make quote + settle bill from it. This makes the already-advertised marketing math (CREDIT_MEANING, the plan cards' "$0.05/enriched lead") the real math, resolves the 1-vs-3 discrepancy in the direction the cards already promise, and — because per-lead prices become integers — fixes the slider bug by construction (per-lead rate = the real rate; "1 credit = 1 scanned lead" becomes true).

Plan adjustments (small, surgical)

  • Rebase Scale: $299 / 24,000 cr ($0.0125/cr) is below the pass-through floor of several SKUs even after repricing. Recommend $299 / 18,000 cr ($0.0166/cr) — same per-credit floor as Growth; headline stays honest: "18,000 credits ≈ 4,500 fully-enriched leads ≈ $0.066/lead."
  • Update plan-card copy from "per enriched lead" ×3 to ×4 (Starter "from $0.084", Growth "$0.066", Scale "from $0.066") — or keep 3-cr full-lead by folding AI research into the scan bundle; either is coherent, pick one and make CREDIT_MEANING, cards, and settle agree.
  • Free tier stays 50 credits = 50 scanned leads or ~12 fully-enriched — still a real "aha," now with honest depletion so free users convert instead of living on 50 credits for months.
  • Market the two hidden differentiators on the pricing page: "Market discovery is always free" and "No charge when a scan fails — you pay for answers, not attempts." Zero code, real positioning vs Apollo's pay-for-misses reputation.

Why not just keep undercutting at cost?

Penetration pricing works when funded to buy share (Origami: $2M seed, verification at cost as a wedge). A solo founder's version of that strategy is: free discovery as the wedge (unmetered, already built, cheap) and healthy margin on enrichment (where the value is proven by the quote the user just accepted). The proposal keeps you the cheapest credible option at every tier while producing ZoomInfo-class margins at face rates and 60–70% at plan rates — enough to fund DataForSEO bills, the free tier, and growth without outside money.
