Mapsly · Portal rework · v3 — “the leads table is the workbench”

UX coverage, the 10 reworks, bulk-first scenarios & the reasoning behind every screen

Companion to the clickable prototype. v3 is a structural rework on top of v2: a Research is its leads, the leads table becomes a power workbench, detail moves into a drawer over that table, touchpoints become a bulk action on leads, and money is expressed in credits everywhere. This doc explains each rework — the problem you flagged, what changed, and why — then re-walks the end-to-end scenarios as the bulk workflow they now are.

✓ Discover → analyze the whole set on the table → bulk act → bulk touch → work the pipeline — one place

The shift & the model

What v2 still got wrong: it spread the work across peer pages — a flat My leads list, a separate Research world, and a top-level Touchpoints world — and it forced a full-page business detail route, so every drill-in lost the list. Money showed as dollars, which made costs feel like billing rather than a meter. And a Research and its leads were two different places when they are really one thing.

The v3 thesis (one sentence): The leads table is the workbench. You discover a market, then you analyze and act on the whole set in bulk — sort, filter, add signal columns, select, and run bulk actions — and detail opens in a drawer over the table so you never lose context. Touchpoints are a bulk action on leads. A Research is its leads. Costs are credits.

The flow (one line): Set up → pick a goal → build with operator+value filters → preview (credits, local scope) → discover → enrich (one-line gate) → workspace: analyze the whole set on the power table → bulk act → bulk-generate touches → work the pipeline → return anytime.

Scope we now say out loud: we map local businesses on Google. “We map local businesses on Google — the ~420 med spas in Miami.” That line appears on welcome, on the discover/preview, and in the workspace header so the scope is unmistakable.

The 10 reworks — problem → change → why

Each block: the problem you flagged, what changed, and the reasoning. All ten are implemented in the prototype.

R1Say what we search: local businesses
Problem
The product never stated what it searches. A new user couldn’t tell if this was lead-gen, a CRM, or a scraper.
Changed
Explicit scope copy on welcome, on discover/preview, and in the workspace header — e.g. “We map local businesses on Google — the ~420 med spas in Miami.”
Why
The scope is the value proposition: a complete, refreshed map of local businesses by signal. Naming it up front sets expectations and frames every count the user sees.
R2Credits-only — no dollar signs in the product
Problem
Dollars everywhere (Discover $0.40, “add $5”, “≈$2.50”) made spending feel like being billed at each step, and mixed the meter with real money.
Changed
Every cost, balance, gate, and ledger entry is in credits shown as a coin (). “Discover this market · 8 credits”, “Enrich 61 leads · 73 credits”, wallet “1,180 credits”. The only place a $ appears is the Billing top-up purchase screen.
Why
Credits are a usage meter, not a checkout. One unit makes costs comparable across discover/enrich/touch and removes the “am I being charged again?” friction at every action.
R3Simplify billing — allowance + top-up; one-line gate
Problem
The enrich gate was a 3-card decision page that stalled the run; billing was a vague Wallet.
Changed
The gate is now a single inline strip: “Enrich 61 leads = 73 credits · you have 50 · [Top up] [Upgrade plan]” with one primary action and a one-line server-enforce note (“a run that can’t be covered won’t start”). Plans give a monthly allowance; a one-time top-up buys extra credits in advance. Billing page = plan card + usage bar (used / remaining this cycle) + one-time top-up (the lone $ price) + a credits-only ledger.
Why
A gate should not be a fork in a flow — it should be a meter reading with a single fix. Allowance-plus-top-up matches how agencies budget: a steady monthly burn plus occasional bursts.
R4Merge “My leads” + “Research” into one workspace
Problem
A flat My leads page and a separate Research world held the same rows twice; a top-level Touchpoints nav split the work into three.
Changed
Nav collapses to Get leads · My research · Billing · Settings. The standalone My leads and top-level Touchpoints are removed. Clicking a research opens its workspace — a research is its leads.
Why
There is no “lead” that isn’t part of some research. One object, one place. Fewer top-level nouns to learn, no duplicate pages to keep in sync.
R5Leads table = the workbench (the biggest rework)
Problem
The leads view was a read-only enriched list. You couldn’t sort, couldn’t add signal columns, couldn’t bulk-act — it answered “here are rows”, not “what do I do next”.
Changed
A power table: sortable columns (click header, arrow shows direction) · a filter bar of removable chips on any field/signal with operator + value (R9), “showing N of M” · a Columns ▾ picker to add/remove columns including signal columns (reviews, rating, Lighthouse, site-age, built-on, ads/no-pixel, 3-pack rank, organic traffic, reachability, match %, status, touch, last-contacted) that show real values color-coded vs the cell · bulk select (header + per-row, “select all N filtered”) feeding a sticky bulk action bar (Generate touches · Set status ▾ · Save to list · Enrich more · Export CSV · Hide) · Group by cell/discovery with collapsible group headers + counts · density toggle (comfortable/compact) + sticky header.
Why
Agencies work sets, not rows. A table that you can shape (sort/filter/columns) and act on (select + bulk) is the fastest path from a discovered market to outreach. Think Linear / Attio / Airtable density.
R6Bulk-first & info-rich — understand the whole set without opening a lead
Problem
To understand a market you had to open leads one by one; the set-level picture lived nowhere.
Changed
Above the table: a compact summary strip (total / reachable / matched / hidden) and a collapsible “Market standards” panel (cell p50/p90 vs your leads). Bulk actions run on the current selection or the whole filtered set.
Why
The table itself should answer “what do I do next.” Cohort counts plus the cell benchmark let the user analyze in bulk and act in bulk — no single-lead spelunking required.
R7Detail = drawer, not a page
Problem
The full-page business detail route navigated away from the table — every drill-in lost the sort/filter/selection you’d built.
Changed
Clicking a row opens a right-side slide-in drawer over the table (table stays mounted underneath). One scroll holds everything: header (name · match · reachability · compliance pill) · Why-qualified vs-cell bars · Reviews · Website & tech + Lighthouse · Ads · Search/SERP · Services + cell prevalence · AI research · Expert findings · Contacts · this lead’s Touches. Prev/next arrows move through the table without closing. Drawer actions: Generate touch · Set status · Save to list. Esc/overlay closes; role="dialog" + focus trap.
Why
Context is the workbench. A drawer keeps the set in view, makes prev/next triage cheap, and removes a whole route from the IA.
R8Touchpoints integrated — not a separate world
Problem
Touchpoints lived in their own top-level nav, disconnected from the leads they’re about.
Changed
(a) A Touchpoints tab inside the workspace (tabs: Leads · Touchpoints · Overview). (b) Touches are generated as a bulk action from selected leads on the Leads tab — a panel for channel, what-you-sell, “generate N drafts.” (c) The Touchpoints tab is a bulk table: each draft = business + channel + predicted-reply + body preview + status (Draft/Queued/Sent/Replied) with row select and a bulk bar (Approve all · Edit · Regenerate · Mark sent · Export CSV) + filters by status/channel. (d) Clicking a draft opens the same lead drawer scrolled to Touches. (e) The Leads table gains a Touch column + status.
Why
A touch is grounded in a lead’s signals; keeping them in the same workspace (and tied via the drawer) preserves the “why” and lets the user generate and approve in bulk.
R9Templates + signals upgrade — operators & values everywhere
Problem
Filters were on/off toggles; you couldn’t say “reviews > 30” or “Lighthouse < 50.” Templates were thin.
Changed
A richer template gallery (8–10: Website redesign, Local SEO, Paid ads, Reputation, Booking-tool SaaS, Social media mgmt, Content/blog, Full-service, Reactivation, Custom) — each card shows who-you-find + preset signals + outcome. Every filter row — in Build, in the table filter bar, and in the signal library — has an operator control (>, ≥, <, ≤, =, between, is/has) + a value input: “reviews > 30”, “Lighthouse < 50”, “rating between 3.5 and 4.5”, “built on = Wix.” Curated SIGNALS (tagged, with a plain-English why) and raw-data fields mix freely; the library keeps its groups + search and uses the same operator+value controls.
Why
Specificity is the moat. Operator+value turns the 90-signal library into a precise query builder so each user can encode exactly the local business they sell to.
R10Flatten — fewer clicks, fewer pages
Problem
Too many nested routes: discover → leads → detail → touchpoints, each a full navigation.
Changed
Researched info is surfaced at table altitude (signal columns) and drawer altitude (one scroll). The standalone full-page detail route is gone (it’s the drawer). The workspace — tabs + drawer — is where the work lives, so the page tree is shorter.
Why
Every removed navigation is saved attention. Flattening to one surface (with tabs and a drawer) is what makes the workbench feel fast.

The workbench model — “analyze the whole set, act in bulk”

The workspace’s main tab is the leads power table. The user reads the set, shapes it, then acts on it — without leaving the table.

Shape the set

  • Sort — click any header; arrow shows direction.
  • Filter bar — removable chips, operator + value, “showing N of M.”
  • Columns ▾ — add/remove columns, including signal columns color-coded vs the cell.
  • Group by — cell/discovery, collapsible headers with counts.
  • Density — comfortable / compact; sticky header.

Act on the set

  • Select — header + per-row checkboxes; “select all N filtered.”
  • Bulk bar — Generate touches · Set status ▾ · Save to list · Enrich more · Export CSV · Hide.
  • Whole-set actions — run on the current selection or the entire filtered set.
  • Drawer — click a row to open detail over the table; prev/next to triage.

Read the set without opening a lead (R6)

Summary strip (total / reachable / matched / hidden) + collapsible Market standards panel (cell p50/p90 vs your leads). The set is legible at a glance; the drawer is for the one lead you decide to write to.

v2 vs v3 — the structural change

✗ v2 — prior prototype

  • Nav: Get leads / Research / My leads / Touchpoints / Wallet.
  • A Research and its leads were two places.
  • Leads view = read-only enriched list; no sort/columns/bulk.
  • Drill-in = full-page detail route → lost the list.
  • Touchpoints = a separate top-level world.
  • Money in dollars at every step; 3-card enrich gate.
  • Filters were on/off toggles (no operator/value).
  • Scope (“local businesses”) never stated.

✓ v3 — this prototype

  • Nav: Get leads · My research · Billing · Settings.
  • A Research is its leads — one workspace.
  • Leads = a power table: sort · filter bar · columns ▾ · group-by · bulk bar · density.
  • Drill-in = a drawer over the table; prev/next; never lose context.
  • Touchpoints = a tab + a bulk action generated from leads.
  • Money in credits () everywhere; gate is one inline strip; $ only on top-up.
  • Every filter has an operator + value (signals + raw fields).
  • “We map local businesses on Google” stated on welcome / preview / workspace.

⏳ Empty / error / loading states

Open product decisions (choices, not gaps)

  1. Credit pricing — the $-per-credit shown on the Billing top-up screen, and the monthly allowance per plan tier (e.g. Growth = 1,600/mo). What are the real numbers?
  2. Default columns — which signal columns are visible on first open of a research (vs hidden behind Columns ▾)? Suggest: match % · reviews · rating · Lighthouse · reachability · status · touch.
  3. Group-by default — single-cell research = flat; multi-cell = grouped by cell. Confirm, or always flat with group-by opt-in?
  4. Saved views — should a shaped table (sort + filter + columns) be saveable/nameable per research (Attio-style views) in v1, or v2?
  5. Touch channels — which channels ship in the bulk-generate panel (email, LinkedIn, call script, DM)? Predicted-reply model per channel.
  6. “Enrich more” semantics — does bulk Enrich-more on the table re-run all signals, or only the missing ones, for already-discovered leads? Affects the credit cost shown.
  7. Plan-tier labels — keep Solo / Growth / Pro / Boutique, or rename for the credits model?

None block the prototype — they’re choices to confirm before we implement the rework in app code.
