Part D

Legal analysis — realistic exposure, not paranoia

Question Answer
Is naming rivals with prices legal? Yes — protected and encouraged. FTC comparative-advertising policy (16 C.F.R. §14.15) encourages truthful named comparisons; trademark use is nominative fair use (New Kids on the Block, Smith v. Chanel). "Closest rival" and "per-seat tax" are non-actionable opinion (Pizza Hut v. Papa John's).
What legal standards apply? Lanham Act §43(a) (literal falsity / misleading implication — only competitors have standing, Lexmark 572 U.S. 118); FTC Act §5 + substantiation doctrine (reasonable basis before dissemination); Canada Competition Act s.74.01 (literal meaning + general impression; AMPs now up to greater of $10M or 3% of worldwide revenue — enforcement against a B2B micro-SaaS is remote).
Does auth-gating help? It lowers exposure, not legality. An in-app billing page is still "commercial advertising" under §43(a). Verified: the price-multiple claims are NOT on the indexed public /compare/* pages (those carry only qualitative capability claims, currently substantiable) — good separation, keep it.
Realistic risk ladder Overwhelmingly nothing → C&D / demand letter if a rival notices (ZoomInfo and Apollo have active legal teams; a letter costs them ~$0) → NAD/BBB challenge (rare at this scale) → Lanham suit (very unlikely; injunction, not damages). The cheap insurance is the substantiation kit in C1-#5.
The actual highest-exposure claim Mapsly's own "per enriched lead $0.05–0.08" — first-party deceptive pricing needs no competitor plaintiff and would arm any rival's C&D ("their own table misstates their own price"). Fix #1 resolves it.
Quebec / fr-CA Minor residual: English-only comparative claims with fr-CA shipping creates modest Bill 96 exposure once Canadian marketing starts; B2B buyers largely fall outside the consumer statute. Log for the Canada push, not for launch.

What to do with this: not re-price — gate. Run B3 as a sensitivity grid before any ad spend, set per-channel kill thresholds (e.g. pause if blended CAC > $150 or 3-mo retention < 70%), gate Solo ads on measured 90-day retention from the first organic cohort, and treat annual prepay as the structural hedge. Also verified: the funnel is leaky as shipped — the marketing surface still shows a 3-up grid (Free/$19/$99) with Solo, the designated advertise tier, entirely absent; "advertise Solo only" can't work until AgPricing.tsx carries the 5-tier ladder.
