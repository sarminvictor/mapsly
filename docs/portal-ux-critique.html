Mapsly · Agency portal · page-by-page scored critique

Every screen, scored before and after — against Linear / Attio / Airtable and against the Mapsly brand.

This is the self-critical companion to the rework. For each of the twelve portal pages I give a before score (1–10 across clarity, transparency, brand-fit, best-practice), name exactly what was wrong — citing concrete elements, not vibes — describe the rework, and set a target. Two dedicated sections then fix the two things that were structurally broken: the signal model (our moat, defined wrong before) and the brand (flat Inter made it look AI-templated).

The honest summary
It worked. It just didn't feel like us, and it sold our moat as a checklist.

Two failures ran through every page. Brand: flat Inter, flat white surfaces, generic SaaS copy ("Build your search") — indistinguishable from a Webflow template, which actively undercuts a product whose pitch is editorial confidence. Substance: we presented raw data as if it were the product, and worse, defined "signals" wrong (Active business = >30 reviews) — collapsing our expert composites into a number any scraper can return. The reworks below raise the average page from 5.4 to a target 9.3.

Before → target, at a glance

Each page scored 1–10 as the mean of four dimensions: Clarity (does the user know what's happening?), Transparency (can they see the recipe / cost / why?), Brand-fit (does it feel like Mapsly, not a template?), Best-practice (vs Linear/Attio/Airtable patterns). Scores are design-completeness of the reworked prototype, not production-validated.

What was wrong
  • The single worst screen — it sold our moat as a checklist. Signals and raw fields were one flat, alphabetical, equal-weight list of toggles. A composite like "Overdue for a redesign" sat next to "review_count" with identical visual weight, so the user couldn't tell expertise from atomic data.
  • Wrong definitions baked in. The library literally defined Active business = >30 reviews. That is a raw threshold, not a signal — it threw away the very logic that makes us defensible, and it's wrong (a business with 200 stale reviews and nothing new in a year is not active).
  • No recipe, no trust. Signals didn't show their inputs. The user had to take "this signal is good" on faith — the opposite of transparency, and it killed the chance to drill in and tune.

Rework
  • Two visually distinct tiers. SIGNALS are primary, prominent, grouped by outcome, value-framed, each rendering name · what-it-means · why-to-pitch · the raw recipe as chips · strength · Add. RAW DATA is demoted to an "Advanced: add a raw filter" expander — compact, lower accent, operator + value.
  • Definitions corrected to the real expert composites (e.g. "Active & growing" = velocity trending up AND a review in 30d AND open-now — never a count). Full list in the signal-model section.
  • Every signal exposes its recipe so the user trusts it and can drill to tune the underlying thresholds.
  • Outcome groups with a value line each, replacing the alphabetical mess.
Best-practice cite
Attio & Linear both lead with curated, opinionated objects and tuck the raw field-level filter behind an "advanced" affordance. We had inverted that — raw fields had equal billing with our expertise. Full design in the signal model.

The signal model — the moat (and what we got wrong)

Why this is the whole game

Apollo and ZoomInfo can return a phone number. They cannot tell you that a business's site loads slow and its reply rate is 0% and a competitor with online booking just moved into the neighborhood. That composite judgment is the product. So the single most damaging thing the old portal did was render our composites as if they were raw fields — and define one of them as a review count. That hands the moat back to every scraper on the market.

The correction in one line
A signal is expert judgment encoded — multiple raw points + thresholds + logic. Raw data is an atomic fact. They are not peers, and the UI must never let them look like peers.
Tier 1 · primary Signal

An expert composite built from multiple raw points + thresholds + logic. It is our expertise. The user cannot change its composition (only optionally tune exposed sensitivity); they can see the raw recipe and why it matters.

In the UI: prominent, grouped by outcome, value-framed, searchable. Each renders name · one-line "what it means" · "why it matters / what to pitch" · the recipe as small chips · a strength/confidence · an Add toggle.

Tier 2 · secondary Raw data

An atomic single-source fact, filterable with operator + value (> >= < <= = between). Deliberately de-emphasized — an "Advanced: add a raw filter" expander, compact, lower visual accent.

Examples: review_count rating review_velocity_30d owner_reply_rate lighthouse_perf lighthouse_a11y cms_platform has_meta_pixel ad_count local_pack_rank organic_traffic_est site_age_years photo_count

✗ Before — signals defined as raw thresholds

  • Active business = >30 reviews — wrong; a count, not a signal
  • Flat alphabetical list, equal weight with raw fields
  • No recipe shown — trust on faith
  • No value framing — "here are 90 things"

✓ After — signals as expert composites

  • Active & growing = velocity ↑ AND review <30d AND open-now
  • Primary tier, grouped by outcome, raw fields demoted
  • Every signal shows its recipe chips + strength
  • Value line per group — "is this valuable to me?" answered

Why the two-tier UI is the moat, not just nice
If signals look like raw fields, the user (and any competitor screenshotting us) reads the product as "filters on scraped data." When signals are primary, recipe-bearing, and outcome-grouped, the product reads as encoded expertise you can't get elsewhere — which is exactly what it is, and exactly what justifies the price.

Templates rework — the reasoning

A template is a named preset bundle of signals (+ optional raw filters) for a goal. It's the on-ramp: it pre-fills the build with the right composites so a new user gets a strong query without learning the library first. Three things were broken and are now fixed:

Problem Why it mattered Fix
Fat cards, won't scale past ~5 We need 10+; the loose grid felt heavy and made the gallery a wall Compact 2-col scannable grid (icon · name · outcome · signal chips · Use), searchable + categorizable
"Custom" buried among cards Power users couldn't find "start from scratch"; it's a distinct intent A separated, always-visible dashed row above the gallery — never a peer of the presets
Cards didn't show the bundle User couldn't judge a template's value before clicking Each card shows its preset signal chips inline, so value is legible at a glance

The live gallery is shown on page 2. It scales to dozens because each entry is one tight row, not a card.

Brand alignment — why the portal must feel like Mapsly, not a template

The #1 fix

Flat Inter on flat white is why the portal looked AI-templated. A product whose landing page leads with confident editorial serif and a yellow-highlighted punch phrase cannot hand the user a portal that looks like a Webflow starter — the discontinuity reads as "the real product is the marketing site." The brand isn't decoration; it's the signal that this tool has a point of view, which is exactly what an agency owner pays for.

  • Brand enters via frosted sticky bar, pill controls, Fraunces titles/counts only

Two altitudes — editorial framing for the moments, crisp tooling for the work — unified by one type / color / component system. The dense table never gets a gradient (it would hurt readability); the framing surfaces never get the spreadsheet treatment (they'd lose the brand).

Reasoning · why this beats the generic template

The generic template is recognizable precisely because it has no opinion: default font, flat surfaces, "Get started" copy. Every choice here is an opinion — an editorial serif, a punch color, an outcome-first sentence — and opinions are what make a tool feel built by people who understand the job. For an agency owner deciding whether to trust us with their prospecting, "this team has taste and a point of view" is itself a conversion argument.
