Touchpoints audit · what we feed the AI, what comes out, and how it scores in 2026

2026-07-07 · v0.19.10 · 6-agent sweep: pipeline + prompts, goal→touch linkage + customization, US/CA compliance, web-sourced 2026 standards, and a live generation test — 11 real drafts generated through the production code path for real enriched leads (US + CA, all tones, sparse edge case; total AI cost $0.0016, zero rows persisted), then adversarially scored against a sourced 2026 rubric. MVP framing honored: no delivery system; email is the priority channel.

1 · Verdict & master scorecard

The architecture is genuinely the right one — the output is held back by four fixable layers above it. The deterministic signal-grounded skeleton + fact-checked nano rewrite produced zero AI hallucinations in 11/11 live samples at $0.005/touch — that grounding discipline is what 2026 explicitly rewards, and most competitors don't have it. But the measured output lands at 6.4/10 against the 2026 rubric because: subject lines leak internal jargon and duplicate across cohorts (3.3/10 — the single biggest lever), the template itself makes two classes of unsupported claims the AI never would, pain ranking ignores what the agency is selling, and the goal the user built at the goal step never reaches the touch at all. Separately, one production blocker: no agency in the DB has a mailing address, so email generation currently produces nothing (every lead reads skippedNoAddress — the live test needed a placeholder to run).

2 · What we feed the AI (the full context)

Two-stage design: a deterministic skeleton (no AI) assembles opener + 1–2 pain lines + close + compliance footer from 14 lead facts; an optional nano rewrite (user-triggered "Polish", 1 cr) makes it fluent. The model never sees raw enrichment data — only the already-grounded sentences.

What never reaches a touch even when the lead has it: AI-brief pain hypotheses / opener angles, pricing transparency, SERP rank & 3-pack, brand hijack, socials, tech stack beyond booking, review quotes. The drawer's "grounded in this lead's signals" is true but covers ~7 of the 40+ signal vocabulary — the moat data mostly stays out of the outreach.

The nano prompts — verbatim nano-fill.ts:56–79 · model gpt-5.4-nano · maxTokens 400 

SYSTEM: You rewrite cold-outreach email bodies so they read like a real person wrote them.

ABSOLUTE RULES — violating any one makes your output useless:
- You may ONLY rephrase the sentences you are given. Reword for fluency and a natural, warm tone.
- NEVER add a fact, a statistic, a number, a price, a percentage, a claim, a guarantee, or a name that is not already in the input.
- NEVER add new sentences that introduce information. …
- Keep every number EXACTLY as written in the input. …
- Keep the business name exactly as written.
- Do NOT add a signature, a footer, links, or a postal address — those are added separately.
- Keep it short. Cold emails that get replies are 3–5 short sentences.

USER: Business name (keep exactly): {name}
Rewrite this email body for fluency, keeping every fact and number exactly as-is: --- {body minus footer} ---

Enforcement is prompt + mechanical post-check (new numbers rejected, fabrication-word blacklist, business-name survival, unfilled tokens) with fallback-to-skeleton on any failure — and a failed call charges $0. Known theoretical gap: the check is token-level, so a meaning-inverting rewrite that keeps the same tokens could pass; live test found zero such cases.

Hallucination audit: the AI layer is clean — the TEMPLATE is the claim risk.

Nano-introduced hallucinations: 0/11 (every number preserved; hedges only; one rewrite improved honesty: "There's no way to book" → "I also couldn't find a way to book"). But two template-embedded unsupported-claim classes shipped: (1) "while neighbors keep climbing" in 5 emails + "vs cell" in subjects — no sample had any competitor/percentile data (null in all 11); (2) exact counts from partial pulls — Zahra's "You have 2 unanswered negative reviews" was computed from an 11-of-607 (1.8%) review sample; the owner checks, finds 5, and the "expert" loses credibility mid-read. Should be percentile-gated and "at least N".

  • Template smell: ~15–20% of body words are lead-specific; the 3 Kelowna review-decline leads share ~85–90% identical skeletons AND identical subjects — Gmail duplicate-fingerprinting risk within a geo cohort even at low volume.
  • Pitch-blind ranking: Zahra's 23.2s LCP and Tommy Gun's 12.4s — spectacular hooks under a literal "website speed fixes" pitch — never appear: slow_site (sev 55) is always outranked by generic review pains (70–80).
  • Sparse lead = spam template: KM Tires (zero signals) produced a grounded-but-generic 31-word note indistinguishable from 2019 spam — and it's exactly where the CASL consent basis is weakest (no website, no published email). Should be gated, not generated.
  • CTA in 10/11: "Want a quick rundown of what I found for {FullLegalName}?" — interest-based (correct per Gong) but identical everywhere and no human types "…for Serenity Aesthetics Laser & Advanced Skin Care Inc?".

5 · Goal → touchpoint: the broken chain, and what Tom can customize

The goal never reaches the touch. P0

generateTouchpointsAction has no goalId — outcome groups ("Reputation", "Weak web"…), tuned signal strictness, and campaign strategy (signalWeights, recommended enrichments) are computed for discovery and then discarded. Tom picks a Reputation goal; his touches cite slow sites and booking gaps with equal weight, and the pain multipicker always offers all 7 themes. Even sellingWhat is re-typed by hand at generation although the campaign already knows it. actions.ts:99–286 · goal-templates OUTCOME_GROUPS · campaign/strategy.ts

Fix: map outcome group → default allowedPainKeys; accept goalId/campaignId and autofill sellingWhat; boost pain severity for themes matching the pitch (the pitch-aware ranking §3).

The strategic read. Mapsly's moat data is exactly what 2026 cold email rewards — the two highest-weight rubric dimensions (personalization + relevance ≈ 30%) are signal problems, and no generic AI-email tool can cite "your site takes 7.7s and 2 negative reviews sit unanswered" with a verified source. The architecture already guarantees the honesty part (0 hallucinations, measured). What's missing is plumbing, not invention: route the goal and the pitch into pain selection, write subjects a human would, and stop the template from claiming things the data doesn't hold. That's 1–2 focused sessions to an output that beats what Tom can buy elsewhere in 2026.
