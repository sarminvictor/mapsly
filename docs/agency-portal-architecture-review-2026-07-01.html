01Executive summary & scorecard

Verdict: a well-architected prototype with production-unsafe seams — specifically the money paths and the failure paths.

The macro-design is right, and rare for a codebase this young: user actions never call an external API (mechanically enforced — the cost counter throws outside a CronRun), all paid work runs on an air-gapped queue drained by a 2-minute cron with a self-chain accelerator, freshness dedup serves repeat work at $0, and the credit wallet has an atomic hold with server-side anti-tamper re-quoting. Happy-path time-to-value beats Apollo-class incumbents: enrichment starts in seconds, progress is polled live, flow state resumes from the URL.

But the rail fails the three table-stakes properties of a production job pipeline: (1) nothing is atomic — jobs are claimed with a plain findMany + unconditional update while overlapping invocations are the design (2-min cron × 300s ticks × self-chain × per-enqueue kicks), so double vendor spend and double credit settlement are routine failure modes, not edge cases; (2) success signals are wrong — workers report failure by return value but the dispatcher bills on "didn't throw", so failed units are charged full price and runs close "OK"; (3) settlement is not crash-safe — a killed tick can close a zero-work run as a phantom success or leak a credit hold forever. On top of that sits a shipped billing-promise gap (plan cards advertise 3.75× the credits the Stripe webhook grants), a confirmed cross-tenant leak (OutreachDraft), and a UX that renders PARTIAL/FAILED runs as 100% success while hard-capping the workbench at 200 of the leads the customer paid for.

None of this is a rewrite. The fixes are one design iteration: a conditional-claim UPDATE, a worker-outcome contract, a transactional close, two indexes, one column (agencyId on OutreachDraft), one constant reconciliation (plan credits), and honest failure states in the UI. P0 below is roughly a week of focused work and removes every money-bleeding defect.

02What is actually built — and the parallel generations

05High-severity findings

15 high findings survived adversarial verification (0 refuted). The two independent "no atomic claim" reports are merged into H6 below, so 14 blocks are shown. Every one is CONFIRMED against the code.

H1Billing cards promise 900/6,000/24,000 monthly credits but the Stripe grant engine deposits 600/1,600/12,000 cost Docs CONFIRMED

Evidence. PLAN_CARDS display Starter $19→900cr, Growth $99→6,000cr, Scale $299→24,000cr (modules/cost/pricing.ts:143-179), mapped via PLAN_TIER_MAP starter→SOLO, growth→GROWTH, scale→BOUTIQUE (pricing.ts:211-216). The Stripe webhook grants PLAN_CREDITS[Agency.plan] = SOLO 600 / GROWTH 1,600 / BOUTIQUE 12,000 (pricing.ts:52-57; modules/cost/server.ts:702; app/api/webhooks/stripe/route.ts:315). The two lattices were 'deliberately decoupled' per the pricing.ts:60-77 comment, but the credit AMOUNTS were never reconciled.

Impact. An agency subscribes to Growth $99 seeing '6,000 credits / month' on team/billing (page.tsx:161 renders PLAN_CARDS); invoice.paid grants 1,600; the wallet pill shows 1,600 available — a 3.75× shortfall on a paid promise, guaranteed support escalation/chargeback the first time any Starter/Growth/Scale customer checks their balance.

Fix. Unify to one lattice: derive PLAN_CREDITS from PLAN_CARDS through PLAN_TIER_MAP (or vice-versa) and add a unit test asserting PLAN_CARDS[k].monthlyCredits === PLAN_CREDITS[PLAN_TIER_MAP[k]] for every paid key. Flag as human-required (payments) per CLAUDE.md before changing grant amounts.

H2Three incompatible credit definitions across docs and code — every documented margin table mis-models actual spend cost Docs CONFIRMED

Evidence. enrichment-cost-model.md:101 says '1 credit = 1 enriched lead' with plans Solo $49/600cr … Boutique $499/12,000cr (lines 103-110, matching PLAN_CREDITS and CLAUDE.md). The billing UI + pricing-strategy.md use '3 credits = 1 fully-enriched lead' with plans Starter $19/Growth $99/Scale $299 (pricing.ts:83-89; docs/pricing-strategy.md:59-61). The runtime charges neither: reconcileRunCredits settles ceil(runUSD/$0.05) (modules/cost/estimate.ts:84-87; dispatch.ts:560-564), so a contacts+reviews lead (~$0.023) costs ~0.5 credits amortized — not 1, not 3. The pipeline doc's 'canonical price list' adds a fourth scheme (per-family fixed credits: contacts 1, ai_research 2, serp 8 — enrichment-pipeline-architecture.html §08) that matches no code.

Impact. Margin/runway tables in enrichment-cost-model.md §4/§6 and pricing-strategy.md §margin are computed from credit-per-lead assumptions the engine doesn't implement; a pricing decision made from any of these docs (e.g. doubling plan credits 'at 50% margin') would be calibrated against fiction. Customers comparing the card's '3 credits per enriched lead' to their actual ledger see unexplained numbers.

Fix. Declare one canonical definition (the usdToCredits engine is what actually bills), encode the per-lead expected credit cost as a tested constant, and regenerate the doc tables from modules/cost/pricing.ts (PRICE_LIST_VERSION already exists for exactly this).

H3Workers signal failure by return value but the dispatcher bills on 'didn't throw' — failed units are marked DONE and charged cost Pipeline CONFIRMED

Evidence. processJob: `await worker(job.businessId)` then unconditional DONE (dispatch.ts:463-469) with costUsd left at the planned price (set at fan-out, dispatch.ts:346-352). Workers that resolve on failure: scanBusinessContacts returns status 'FAILED' on fetch failure (modules/contacts/scan.ts:313-335); submitReviewJob returns a FAILED ReviewJob for no-googleCid and for task_post-failed-after-retries (modules/reviews/review-job.ts:224-245, 275-300); enrichLighthouseForBusinesses counts failed:1 and returns (modules/discovery/enrich-lighthouse.ts:235-253).

Impact. A business whose site times out once, or that has no googleCid, produces a DONE EnrichmentJob billed at full planned price ($0.008 contacts / $0.015 reviews / $0.00425 lighthouse). closeRunIfDone sees zero FAILED jobs, closes the run OK, and settleRun charges the full amount — the agency pays credits for zero data and the run reports success.

Fix. Make processJob inspect worker outcomes: define a common WorkerResult { ok, billable } per family (contacts FAILED/SKIPPED → throw or return billable:false; review FAILED job → billable:false; lighthouse failed/openAudited=0 → billable:false). On non-billable outcomes set EnrichmentJob costUsd=0 and status FAILED (or SKIPPED) so retries engage and settle refunds the unit.

H4A FAILED AdMarketRun satisfies the collectors' freshness gate — one vendor hiccup locks a cell out of meta/google/serp data for 30 days while fan-out keeps billing cost Pipeline CONFIRMED

Evidence. latestAdMarketRun does not filter status (freshness.ts:47-57), so the FAILED telemetry row written on vendor failure (meta-ads.ts:185-195, google-ads.ts:133-143, serp.ts:146-156) gates the next attempt as 'served-from-db' for 30 days. Meanwhile fanOutRun's gate DOES filter to OK/PARTIAL (enrich-fresh-db.ts:70-75), so it re-invokes the collector and adds the family price to cellCostUsd whenever the call doesn't throw (dispatch.ts:283-292) — and 'served-from-db' doesn't throw.

Impact. Concrete sequence: Apify Meta run 500s → FAILED AdMarketRun written. User re-runs meta_ads for the cell next day: quote bills $0.05 (fan-out sees no OK/PARTIAL run), runMetaAdsForCell returns 'served-from-db' off the FAILED row, user is charged 1 credit for zero data — repeatable for 30 days.

Fix. Filter latestAdMarketRun to status IN ('OK','PARTIAL') so FAILED runs never gate; and make fanOutRun bill from the collector's returned outcome/costUsd (only outcome==='collected' bills) instead of price-on-no-throw.

H5Enrichment dispatcher has no atomic job claim or run-close lock; overlapping invocations double-process jobs and double-settle credits cost Security CONFIRMED

Independently confirmed by 4 reviewers (job-rail, adapters, pipeline, security).

Evidence. dispatchPending selects QUEUED jobs with a plain `findMany({ where: { status: 'QUEUED' }, take: JOB_BATCH*3 })` and processes them (modules/enrichment/dispatch.ts:743-792); processJob flips a job to RUNNING with an UNCONDITIONAL update (no `WHERE status='QUEUED'` guard) before running the worker (dispatch.ts:458-464). closeRunIfDone guards only on a non-transactional read of run.status==='RUNNING' and then does seconds of playbook work before writing OK/PARTIAL and calling settleRun (dispatch.ts:507-564); settleRun's idempotency relies on read-then-write ledgerSum(SETTLE/REFUND) with no lock (modules/cost/server.ts:516-596). Concurrent invocations are the intended mode: the */2 cron, a fire-and-forget kickDispatch after every enqueue (modules/discovery/enrich-actions.ts:366, modules/discovery/actions.ts:604), and the route's after(kickDispatch) self-chain (app/api/cron/internal/dispatch/route.ts:52-58) can all overlap with no mutex.

Impact. When a user enqueues while a cron/self-chain drain is in flight, two dispatchPending runs select the same QUEUED job and both invoke the paid worker (Apify DOM actor ~$0.06, DfS Lighthouse, gpt AI stages), double-spending the enrichment budget the cost-discipline rules call non-negotiable. Likewise two closeRunIfDone calls that both read RUNNING within the multi-second playbook window each call settleRun, creating duplicate SETTLE ledger rows and double-decrementing wallet buckets — over-charging the agency's credits.

Fix. Claim jobs atomically (updateMany `WHERE status='QUEUED'` to a CLAIMED/RUNNING state returning affected ids, or `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`), make processJob's RUNNING transition conditional on current QUEUED, and guard run close/settle with a Postgres advisory lock or a conditional `updateMany({where:{id,status:'RUNNING'},data:{status:'CLOSING'}})` so only the winner settles. A single dispatch mutex (advisory lock per tick) also suffices.

H6OutreachDraft rows are not agency-scoped — agencies in overlapping markets read and edit each other's outreach content security Security CONFIRMED

Evidence. OutreachDraft has no agencyId column (prisma/schema.prisma:2610-2626). lead-detail.ts loads drafts with `prisma.outreachDraft.findMany({ where: { businessId } })` — no agency filter — after gating the business only on shared cellKey market data (modules/agency-portal/discover/lead-detail.ts:280,351-352). The mutation guard callerOwnsDraft authorizes any draft whose business.cellKey is in ANY of the caller's discovered cells (modules/agency-portal/discover/workbench-actions.ts:56-85), and saveTouchBodyAction/setTouchSentAction then update body/subject/status (workbench-actions.ts:107-115,149-152). Drafts are created per-business with no agency link (modules/outreach/generate.ts:228-244).

Impact. Two agencies that both discover the same cell (e.g. 'med spa / Miami' — the core, expected product usage) share the same Business rows. Agency A's lead drawer renders every OutreachDraft for that business, including Agency B's subject/body containing B's proprietary pitch and 'sellingWhat' positioning; Agency A can also overwrite B's draft text or flip its status to 'sent'. A competitor agency thus exfiltrates and tampers with another tenant's outreach strategy through normal UI actions.

Fix. Add agencyId to OutreachDraft (backfill from the generating agency), filter lead-detail's draft load by agencyId, and change callerOwnsDraft to require draft.agencyId === caller's agencyId instead of shared-cell membership.

H7Tick killed during fanOutRun's inline cell phase closes the run as a phantom 'OK' with zero work done stability Job rail CONFIRMED

Evidence. fanOutRun flips the run RUNNING at dispatch.ts:262-265, then runs per-cell families inline and sequentially (meta_ads is an Apify actor call, up to ~295s/cell — dispatch.ts:279-293, cell-intel/meta-ads.ts:19), and only creates EnrichmentJob rows afterwards (createMany at dispatch.ts:356). The function budget is 300s (vercel.json). closeRunIfDone closes any RUNNING run whose open-job count is 0 (dispatch.ts:511-514) and sets status OK when there are no FAILED jobs (dispatch.ts:553).

Impact. A 3-cell run with meta_ads+google_ads+serp can burn the entire 300s inside the cell loop; the invocation is killed before createMany. Next tick: the run is RUNNING with zero jobs → open=0 → closeRunIfDone marks it OK, unitsCompleted=0, refunds the hold (hadProgress=false path), and recomputes cells. The agency sees a completed run in the tray/Enriching page while none of the per-business enrichment they requested ever executed, and nothing will retry it.

Fix. Create the EnrichmentJob rows (or at least a 'fanned_out' marker on the run) BEFORE the inline cell-family calls, or move per-cell families into their own job rows so fan-out is a fast pure-DB step; alternatively have closeRunIfDone refuse to close a RUNNING run with zero job rows unless the plan was genuinely cell-only.

H8Pingback path skips the depth-escalation ladder — high-velocity businesses get silently truncated review windows stability Pipeline CONFIRMED

Fix. Route the pingback through the same persistFetchResult path used by fetchReviewJob (it already handles cursor update + aggregates + escalation), or replicate the shouldEscalate check in the webhook and re-submit the deeper task before marking DONE.

H9fanOutRun is not crash-safe: RUNNING flip precedes slow inline cell collectors and job creation — a mid-fan-out crash yields a zero-job run that closes 'OK' stability Pipeline CONFIRMED

Evidence. The run flips RUNNING first (dispatch.ts:262-265), then per-cell collectors run inline and sequentially (279-293; a Meta Apify run alone can take a minute+), then jobs are created (356-358). fanOutRun is guarded to only fan out PENDING runs (248), and reconcileStuck only recovers jobs and discoveries (664-679) — never a half-fanned run. closeRunIfDone on a zero-job RUNNING run sees open=0, done=0, totalUsd=0 → closes OK with hadProgress=false → full refund (507-564).

Impact. If the 300s dispatch invocation is killed mid-fan-out (realistic: discoveries with up-to-3000-listing paginated fetches run first in the same tick — 703-712), the run permanently loses its jobs: next tick closes it 'OK' with unitsCompleted=0. The agency sees a completed run that enriched nothing (credits refunded, but vendor money already spent on any completed cell collectors and the UX reports success).

Fix. Make fan-out resumable: keep the run PENDING until jobs are created (create jobs idempotently keyed on runId+businessId+family, then flip RUNNING), or add a FANNING intermediate status that reconcileStuck resets to PENDING after N minutes. Move inline cell collectors into their own EnrichmentJob rows (unit='cell') so they get retry/isolation too.

H10REVIEWS jobs are DONE and billed at task submission; run closes and settles before any review data lands architecture Pipeline CONFIRMED

Evidence. If the DfS task is lost (24h ceiling), the agency paid $0.015/business for nothing with no refund path.

Fix. Either keep the REVIEWS EnrichmentJob non-terminal until the ReviewJob reaches DONE (reconcile the two state machines in the dispatch tick), or bill reviews on data-landing (pingback/reconcile settles a per-unit micro-charge). Trigger runPlaybooksForBusiness again from the pingback handler for businesses touched by an open/recent run.

11What is genuinely good — keep it

  • Mechanically-enforced cost boundary. No external call can run in a user request path — withCostCounter/incrementCost throw outside an open CronRun. This is the strongest single design decision in the system.
  • Credit-wallet primitives. Atomic conditional-UPDATE hold (closes the oversell race), server-side re-quoted single-use estimates (anti-tamper), clamp-at-hold settle that can never over-charge the reservation. The close just needs to be as atomic as the hold.
  • Adapter discipline. Shared DfS transport with timeouts, jittered retries, 429 classification and pacing; KV dedup with explicit TTLs; Zod at the boundary; metered Apify billing with partial-dataset salvage.
  • Freshness dedup at quote AND process time. Repeat work is served at $0 — a real cost advantage over per-record-priced competitors.
  • Route-level multi-tenancy. Every reviewed route/action resolves agencyId from the session (never params) and re-checks resource ownership; internal crons require Bearer CRON_SECRET.
  • UX time-to-value scaffolding. URL-first resumable flow, $0 auto-discovery with live counts, near-instant start, honest per-run stage checklist. Best-in-class for the stage.

Add both to the browser-validation checklist (the repo's rules already mandate a 380px pass on every UI task, so this is enforcement, not new process).
Why: The retention loop everyone agrees to build (digest email) dead-ends if its click destination is broken on the device it's opened on; the fix is CSS on two views, not a mobile strategy.
laterMOutcome feedback loop: won-deal → signal-efficacy data asset
The workbench tracks lead status (contacted/replied/won implied in the prototype's status tabs) but nothing closes the loop from outcomes back to which signals fired — which is simultaneously (a) Tom's ROI proof ('leads flagged ads-without-pixel replied 3.1× more'), (b) Mapsly's only path to tuning the 48-signal weights on real evidence instead of intuition, and (c) a compounding data moat no scraper can replicate because they have no workflow layer. The prototype even mocked this (signal-correlation panel in list analytics per the agency UX rules), so the concept is validated. MVP is deliberately thin: persist status transitions with timestamps (mostly exists), then one aggregate query + one card on the research page once an agency has ≥30 status-changed leads — 'signals that predicted replies in your market.' No ML, no dashboards; a single honest correlation table. Claim it in the brief as the long-term data asset the credit revenue is buying.
Why: It's the only item on this list that makes the product BETTER the more it's used — the moat competitive analysis promised needs an evidence flywheel, and status data is already being collected for free.
laterMChurn, dunning, and the permanence promise: offboarding policy + full-fidelity export
'Paid results are permanent · re-open for $0' collides head-on with cancellation and nobody has reconciled them: does a canceled Growth agency keep its researches? (Trust-consistent answer, and the recommended one: yes — read-only access to purchased researches survives downgrade to free forever; it costs Mapsly ~nothing in COGS and makes the permanence promise real, which in turn makes future credit purchases feel safe.) The brief also needs: Stripe dunning behavior (failed payment → grace period → read-only, never deletion), what happens to in-flight enrichment runs and held credits at cancellation (refund holds, complete or kill running jobs — the ledger rails support this), and a full-fidelity export (JSON with signals, evidence, whyJson, contacts — not the current raw CSV) available at all times, because 'you can leave with everything you paid for' is both a CCPA-adjacent hygiene factor and the single best answer to enterprise-ish procurement objections about lock-in.
Why: The permanence promise is a headline trust feature; leaving its cancellation semantics undefined means the first churned customer discovers the answer in production and tweets about it.

The one-paragraph verdict

Mapsly's agency portal is two workflow wires and one signup route away from being a genuinely differentiated product in a field where every incumbent is either LinkedIn-shaped, inbound-only, or a raw scraper. The prototype already contains a world-class product spec — its trust architecture and evidence-first design are better thinking than what Apollo, Clay, or GoHighLevel ship today — and the live code has already faithfully built the hard front half of it. The MVP is not more features: it is opening the front door (self-serve signup), letting small wallets succeed (top-N enrich), making paid work visible as it lands (live workbench on the DO worker), and letting the evidence leave the building (touches, Proof Pack, rich CSV). Everything else in this report is compounding, not blocking.
