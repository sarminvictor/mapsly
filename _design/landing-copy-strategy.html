The rule that makes it scale

Every recipient has a best pillar and a worst pillar. Open on their real strength, land the one sting on their real weakest spot, resolve into their real fix list. The arc holds whether they're #1 or #22 — because it's built from their data, not Maria's.

How the personalization works

Two layers. The first is set once per campaign; the second flexes for every row in the list.

① Campaign tokens — fixed for the whole Miami send

  • {{category}} / {{categoryPlural}} — "med spa" / "med spas"
  • {{city}} — "Miami"
  • {{total}} — market size, e.g. 26. Used as honest authority + FOMO: "we mapped all 26."
  • {{noun}} — the customer word, chosen by category (see table). This is the answer to "patients vs customers": adapt it, never hardcode.

② Recipient branches — vary per business row

  • Rank tier — leader / top / upper-half / lower-half / unranked → reframes the hero (always positive-or-opportunity, never "you're failing").
  • Ads status — runs ads vs not, market ad-heavy vs open lane → 3 different ad-screen stories.
  • Per-pillar strength — each section (Search, Ads, Reviews, Website) detects strong-vs-weak for this recipient and frames itself as a win bigger or a gap.
  • Biggest gap + fix list — computed from their lowest pillar; powers the hero "blind spot" and the Fixes plan.

The customer noun — by category, with a safe default

Store it on each category (a customerNoun field on BusinessCategory), with a keyword fallback. Recommendation: "patients" for medical only; "clients" is the warm default for the salons / spas / wellness Mapsly mostly serves; "customers" is the absolute safe fallback.

Why a system beats one perfect landing

  • It's true for everyone. Elena's website is great — the template praises it instead of inventing a "leak." Her sting lands where it's real (fewer reviews than the spas above her). Honesty scales; flattery and false alarms don't.
  • "We mapped all 26" is an asset, not a secret. Stating the market-wide measurement up front is authority + FOMO — every owner wants to know where they rank when everyone's being scored. It also makes the personalization believable: this is data, not a hand-written compliment.
  • The noun travels. Swap the campaign to Miami nail salons and every "patients" becomes "clients" automatically. One engine, every local category.
  • It stays honest. No invented proof (Mapsly is pre-launch — no testimonials). Belief comes from their own verifiable numbers, the full-market authority, and a loud money-back guarantee.
  • No free week — the page is the trial. The personalized report above is the sample. We give real, specific value first and only then ask — so reciprocity is already banked and the "does it actually work?" doubt is half-answered before she ever sees the price. Risk is reversed by money-back, not a giveaway.

To ship this: the section copy moves from static strings into a computed copy object built server-side in getLandingData — a nounFor(category) helper plus the tier flags already implied by the data, with safe fallbacks (unranked, no website, brand-new).
